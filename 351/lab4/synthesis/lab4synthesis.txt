4) Effect on number of misses: Increase

   Explain: The extra misses occur in the first `l`-loop, when we are loading a
            block from `A` into our `tmp` array. Since `A` does not start at
            the beginning of a cache block, we will incur 2 misses, rather than
            1, per `BLOCK_SIZE` of `A`. I.e. the `l`-loop now traverses across
            the boundary between two blocks, causing an extra miss.

5) Effect on number of misses: Decrease

   Explain: The main problem with the power-of-2 matrix sizes was that we
            couldn't fully utilise the space in our cache. Since the cache was
            direct-mapped, operations were frequently "fighting" for the same
            sets in the cache, leading to many conflict misses.
            However, with dimensions of 57 and 71, the rows are now
            - 71 * 4 bytes = 284 bytes
            - 57 * 4 bytes = 228 bytes
            These aren't powers of 2, which means a greater fraction of our
            cache is in use at any slice of time. This means less evictions of
            useful data, resulting in fewer misses.
