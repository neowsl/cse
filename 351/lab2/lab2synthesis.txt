4) Strategy + example:

For this lab, there were 2 strategies that were really useful for my workflow.

One was to simply step through the assembly with `si`/`ni` until I hit some sort
of validation structure (like a compare + jump). Then I would examine the
registers using `x` or `p` to determine what input I needed to defuse the bomb.

Another strategy was to translate the assembly instructions to C code, then
reason about it at a high level. For instance, this was super useful for the
recursion task (phase 4). Since the code was short, it was easier to directly
translate it to C. I would recommend this strategy of reasoning in C to future
students.

Lastly, `layout split` was a game-changer, as I was constantly retyping `disas`
to check my location in the assembly. I wish I'd known the command sooner! I
would highly recommend that command to a future 351 student.

5) Difficulty + why (past experience):
-----------------------------------------------------------

I think the most difficult part of assembly is the fact that operations are so
"unrestricted", e.g. directly referencing memory by a numerical address.
Assembly is "correct" in the sense that the compiler that generates it is
well-written, not because the language itself has guardrails. Coming from a
background of Rust/Haskell, where type and memory safety are built-in language
constructs, the free-form nature of assembly definitely took some getting used
to.
